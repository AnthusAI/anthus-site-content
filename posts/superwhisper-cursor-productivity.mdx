Pro tip: When working with Cursor's agent mode or other AI coding assistants, you naturally shift from being a hands-on coder to more of a manager role. Instead of typing symbols and syntax, you spend more time expressing ideas and providing high-level instructions and feedback.

While macOS's built-in dictation is capable, [SuperWhisper](https://superwhisper.com/) goes further: it uses an LLM to review and refine transcriptions, making it accurate for technical discussions. That accuracy means you can keep working with coding agents even when your hands are occupied—cooking breakfast, taking notes on a walk, whatever.

The workflow is simple:
1. Speak your thoughts and instructions
2. SuperWhisper converts and refines them to text
3. Cursor's AI agents interpret and execute your commands

It frees you to focus on architectural decisions and creative problem-solving while the agents handle implementation details—a development process that adapts to your context rather than demanding your full attention at the keyboard.

But hands-free coding is just the start. Once you're comfortable directing development through voice, you can scale up: imagine maintaining multiple feature branches simultaneously, each with its own planning documents to maintain context. While your agents implement one feature, you're free to architect the next, keeping multiple development streams flowing in parallel.

After a lot of hours pair programming with AI agents, it's refreshing to find a tool that makes the experience more natural.
