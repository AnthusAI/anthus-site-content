## A working system they didn't own

Call Criteria runs quality assurance for call centers: human experts listen to calls and score them against detailed scorecards, one scorecard per client. By 2023 they'd already put AI on the problem. An outside scoring service ran custom fine-tuned LLM classifiers against their calls, and it worked. The trouble was everything around it. Every new client scorecard, every retrain, every question about why a score came out the way it did went through a vendor. The classifiers were theirs in name and someone else's in practice, and the business they wanted to grow was sitting on a platform they couldn't see into or scale.

## The choice

They came to us with one ask: bring the technology in-house so they could scale it up. Not a better model. The lab.

We built them an in-house classifier lab on [Plexus](/platform/plexus), our MLOps platform, deployed in their own AWS account so the models, the data, and the record belonged to them. Then we operated it for them, for years. That second part is the engagement: a classifier lab is a running thing, and the value comes from running it well month after month.

## What running it looked like

The lab turned QA into a loop rather than a queue.

1. A classifier scores each call against its client's scorecard.
2. Human experts review a subset, agree or disagree, and say why.
3. Every disagreement is a labelled example the model didn't have, aimed at exactly the cases it was least sure about.
4. The explanations become stated policy the system applies from then on, and the model refits on the corrections.
5. A new version serves only when it clears held-out review on the slices that matter, with a record of what changed and who approved it.

That is reinforcement learning from human feedback as a daily operating procedure, and a data flywheel in the plain sense: the more the reviewers corrected, the less they had to.

Onboarding changed too. A new client scorecard used to take weeks of manual configuration. The lab reads the client's scoring criteria, generates a first classifier tuned to them, deploys it in days, and starts the loop immediately.

## What it produced

- **Hundreds of scorecards** running at once, one per client, each with its own gate and its own reviewers.
- **Millions of calls** scored, within minutes of the call ending instead of days later.
- **Costs that stopped tracking volume.** AI review costs a fraction of human-only review, so growth no longer meant proportional QA headcount.
- **Consistency** across evaluators, because the scoring criteria are applied the same way to every call.
- **Accuracy that climbed month over month**, on the record, version by version.

The reviewers didn't go away, and that was the design. Their judgment is what the system learns from; the lab's job is to spend their attention where it teaches the model the most.

## What it taught us

The lab we built for Call Criteria became the product we run for everyone.
